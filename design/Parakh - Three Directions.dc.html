Three ways to earn a buyer’s trust in 150 numbers

One sourcing event — 30 corrugated box lines, five vendors, five reply shapes — built three times. Same data, same principle: AI reads, code calculates, independent checks verify, the buyer decides. The directions differ in what sits at the centre of the screen.

Every prototype is clickable. Click any number to open its source. All five vendors, prices, doubts and the scenario totals come from one dataset, so the three directions agree to the paisa.

A

Ledger

Comparison-first. The grid is the page; the source and the analyst live in its margins. Cell state is set in type — like a well-kept ledger — and colour is spent on one thing only: a doubt.

Cell states 11.20read & verified — plain ink 11.24converted — dotted rule, a sum lies under it 13.66LYlast year — italic, the quotation voice 5.79↓outside should-cost — arrow —not quoted 18.30?doubt — the only fill 10.88lowest on the line — weight
Source panel opens as a right margin; the grid narrows its spec column but keeps all five vendors visible. Analyst is a query line docked under the grid. Answers rewrite the grid in place, with the rules and exclusions in a strip above it.
Good at — the daily scan. Fastest to see a whole line and a whole vendor at once; prints and exports cleanly; calmest grid of the three. Trades off — typographic marks are subtle, so they need learning; the chat history is thin, so the reasoning behind an answer is less visible to the VP.

B

Brief

Chat-first. The conversation is the spine; whatever it cites opens as a numbered exhibit beside it. Sources arrive in the thread as cited excerpts, so the reasoning and the evidence sit in one column.

Cell states — exceptions only 11.20plain number — read and checked headerconverted — stated once per column (“all USD → ₹”) 13.66last year — glyph 5.79outside should-cost — glyph —not quoted 18.30doubt — magenta glyph 10.88lowest — the only fill
Source panel is the thread itself: clicking a number posts the source card into the conversation. The exhibit never gets covered. Analyst is native — the answer is a message with its rules, exclusions, and buttons for chart and export; the exhibit becomes Exhibit 4.
Good at — explaining. Best for the VP’s what-ifs and for an award he can defend: every answer carries its own audit trail, in order. Trades off — the grid gets ~60% of the width, and the thread grows long; finding an earlier source means scrolling the conversation.

C

Docket

Review queue. The buyer doesn’t scan 150 cells — he clears a docket of five, ranked by rupees at stake, each with its evidence and a drafted action. The grid becomes a map of where the work stands.

Cell states — printed textures read & verified — bare paper converted — flat tint last year — halftone screen outside should-cost — corner notch not quoted — hatching doubt — the one ink colour 10.88lowest — underscored
Source panel is a light table under the grid — documents are wide, so they get the full width, with the arithmetic beside them. Analyst is a scenario desk on the right: the question becomes rules you can toggle, and the grid follows without re-asking.
Good at — making sure nothing is missed. Textures read at map scale (30×5 at 18 px) and every doubt ends in a recorded decision. Trades off — linear and slower for an overview; textured cells are the busiest grid of the three; it assumes the ranking is right.

The data underneath

Sahyadri Appliances, Chakan plant. 30 RSC lines (3- and 5-ply, B, C, E and BC flute, BF 14–28), 5,000 to 1,00,000 boxes each. Should-cost comes from board weight × ₹41/kg (3-ply) or ₹44/kg (5-ply), plus print. Rohit Box’s per-kg email is converted with the same weight formula; Vardhman’s USD at the RBI reference rate; Kaveri’s per-100 prices with their freight added. Shree Balaji’s revised quote supersedes four lines; Anand left out three; last year’s award (SE-2025-037) resolves “same as last year”.
